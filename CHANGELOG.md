# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/). Entries before v2.1.0 are reconstructed from the
git history; `git log` is the complete record.

## [Unreleased]

### Fixed
- `crci-node --replay-window` accepted any value; one above what a peer record can hold overflowed or
  tried to allocate without limit on the first message. Values above 1 024 are now refused at start-up.

### Changed
- Per-node state is bounded: origins (896, the least active forgotten first), the seen cache (1 792 entries, was
  5 000), recent messages (1 024), rescue messages (256), transport peers (1 024, banned peers kept longest,
  where the old pruning deleted exactly those), sync sessions, the Merkle chain (the newest blocks), relay queues
  and the application pipeline. Replay windows hold one bit per sequence number per peer, and peer identities
  are kept as keyed hashes. The threat model states what forgetting costs.
- Log output is bounded per node: frames dropped by a budget print at most 5 lines per second per sender or
  origin; the TCP node's `received message` and `accepted` lines are shared fairly between senders above 1 000
  per 10 s (each sender keeps at least 20); a printed line names the lines held back before it. The runtime's
  line per delivered message and the pipeline's `Accept` verdict line are no longer printed by the TCP node.

### Added
- Fuzz targets (`fuzz/`, `make fuzz`) for the frame decoders, the replay filter (against a reference model),
  a whole node, the sybil guard and the log gates.
- `examples/state_flood`, `make state`: one node's heap under floods of a million signed messages.
- `examples/tcp_flood`, `scripts/testbed/run_tcp_flood.sh`, `make tcp-flood`: a flood of validly signed messages
  from many identities through a real `crci-node`, with its memory, CPU and log output sampled.
- TLA+ models of the final protocol: `docs/tla/Reputation.tla` (strikes, bans, recovery, damping) and
  `docs/tla/CRCI.tla` (seen cache, replay window, strikes, signatures, anti-entropy, an adversary that
  replays, forges, reuses IDs and omits). `docs/tla/check.py` / `make tla` runs every configuration,
  including ones that must fail because an assumption or a defence was removed. Two tests tie the models
  to the code: every transition of the reputation state graph is replayed through `PeerReputation`, and
  behaviours generated by TLC are replayed through real `NodeRuntime` instances, comparing every node's
  state after every step. CI runs the quick checks and fails if the committed exports are stale.
- Emulation testbed (`scripts/testbed/`): real `crci-node` processes in Linux network namespaces with
  netem delay, random geometric and random 4-regular topologies, a replaying relay, per-run analysis and
  fidelity checks.
- `crci-node` experiment options: `--emit-count`, `--emit-interval-ms`, `--emit-delay-ms` (periodic
  signed messages), `--replay-after-ms`, `--replay-copies` (fault injection) and
  `--experiment-epoch-unix-ms` (a shared time origin for those schedules). Origination, acceptance
  and bans are logged with a Unix-millisecond timestamp. `--sender-refill-per-sec` sets the per-neighbour
  budget refill for experiments. Accepted messages are logged with their path (gossip or sync answer) and
  the protocol compute time; relay frames dropped by a failed delivery or a full queue are counted.
- Testbed attacker and recovery experiments (`scripts/testbed/run_attacks.sh`, `make attacks`): an
  over-budget replayer, a fresh-message flood and the 20 % loss condition with a 60 s drain; testbed
  options `--byz-rate` and `--drain`; the analysis reports honest-only delivery, honest senders' frames
  dropped by the rate limit, per-origin budget drops, and aggregates the loss runs per condition. Every
  testbed configuration runs with three seeds, and the load set covers 16 and 32 messages/s.

- `scripts/testbed/main_table.py` regenerates the main testbed table of `docs/benchmarks.md` from `results.csv`;
  `make tables` builds it and the netem tables, `make smoke` runs a small check, `make reproduce` runs every
  experiment and then the tables.

- In-process simulation (`crates/crci-node/examples/mesh_sim`, `scripts/sim/`, `make sim`): unmodified
  `NodeRuntime` instances on the testbed's topologies, up to 5 000 nodes and 45 % replaying attackers,
  cross-checked against the testbed's runs.
- Invalid-signature flood experiment (`scripts/testbed/run_flood.sh`, `flood_table.py`, `make flood`):
  `crci-node --corrupt-signatures` (and `--corrupt-for-ms`) makes a node forge the signature of everything it
  sends from its fault on; the testbed analysis reports process CPU seconds per role. Results:
  [benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md#invalid-signature-flood).
- `TcpTransport::shun_peer`: the node's way to tell the transport that an authenticated identity is banned.
- `TcpTransport::paced_frames` and a `frames paced: total=N` line from `crci-node` (counted by the testbed
  analysis); `TcpTransport::bind_with_pacing` for tests.

- Baselines and ablations (`crci-node --defence`, `--strikes-to-ban`, `--replay-window`; the same switches in
  the `mesh_sim` scenarios; `scripts/testbed/run_baselines.sh`, `scripts/sim/run_ablation.py`,
  `make baselines`, `make ablation`): the `static-bucket` and `vanilla` defence modes, the strike threshold k,
  an injected duplicate fault for honest nodes in the simulation, and a window ablation in `ablation_bench`
  (delivery under reordering and replay detection for W = 0 to 128, and the strike threshold against a paced
  attacker). Results: [benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md#baselines-and-ablations).
- `SybilGuard::set_strikes_to_ban(k)`, `NodeRuntime::set_defence_mode` and `DefenceMode` (experiments only).

- Link-flapping experiment (`scripts/testbed/run_flapping.sh`, `flap_table.py`, `make flapping`; testbed options
  `--flap-fraction`, `--flap-mode`, `--flap-min-s`, `--flap-max-s`, `--flap-start`, `--flap-stop`): a seeded share
  of the overlay links drops packets for 5–10 s at a time, symmetrically or in one direction, and the analysis
  reports bans, strikes, sync requests and delivery per run. Results:
  [benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md#link-flapping).
- Byzantine-ratio experiment (`scripts/testbed/run_ratio.sh`, `ratio_table.py`, `make ratio`; testbed option
  `--attackers-pct`): a seeded share of the nodes replays from 20 s on without colluding, the analysis judges
  bans between honest observers and reports the honest nodes' components after the attackers are removed.
  `scripts/sim/partition_prob.py` computes the probability of a partition over many topology instances and
  checks it against the simulation. Results: [benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md#share-of-replaying-nodes).
- Four evaluation figures in `docs/figures/` (`scripts/figures/make_figures.py`, `make figures`): latency against
  N with 0, 10 and 30 % of the nodes replaying, the reputation of a paced attacker, survivability against packet
  loss, and goodput against the baselines. Every figure is drawn from committed data, states whether it is
  emulation or simulation, and has error bars over seeds where there is any variation. The plot helper gained
  error bars and a numeric-axis chart. [benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md#figures).

### Changed
- The listener reads at most 200 frames per second from one connection (burst 400) and waits when a sender
  goes faster, so TCP flow control slows it. Before, a neighbour that was never struck (a flood of fresh
  valid messages above its budget) made each of its neighbours read, parse and verify every frame it could
  send: 6.3 CPU seconds and 174 MiB of log per neighbour per minute at 1 000 messages/s, against 2.0 s and
  68 MiB now. Honest links never reach the cap.
- A banned peer's connection is closed and its frames are no longer read: the listener refuses its
  connections (after the handshake) for 30 s at a time and lets one probe frame through after that, so a
  readmission is still noticed. Before, every frame of a banned peer was still read, signature-checked
  and parsed (about 60 µs, 6.5 CPU seconds per neighbour in a 2 560 frames/s flood).
- A `crci-node --listen` node no longer logs `SYBIL GUARD DROP … is banned` for every frame of a banned peer
  (a flood turned that into 300 MiB of log in one minute and could stall the receive loop on a blocked
  output): it logs `dropping frames from banned peer …` once per ban and, every 5 s while it changes,
  `banned-peer frames dropped: total=N`.
- The per-neighbour budget refills at 20 messages per second instead of 5, and only messages the
  receiver has not seen spend it. Chosen from a testbed sweep (refill 5/10/20/50 per second, offered load
  4–32 messages/s): 20 is the lowest rate with no honest frame dropped at 16 messages/s. Before, the
  network as a whole could sustain about 5 messages per second.
- `scripts/testbed/README.md` is stored as UTF-8 (two characters were in a legacy encoding).
- Documentation rewritten to describe the code as it is: two message paths (signed `NodeRuntime` vs.
  unsigned TCP), the real transport (custom TCP, no libp2p/Noise), the real TLA+ model scope, and every
  benchmark with its command and raw data. Known gaps are listed in `docs/threat-model.md`.
- `docs/openapi.json` now describes all 15 HTTP/WebSocket routes; `docs/openapi.yaml` removed.
- `docs/design-decisions.md` folded into ADR 004 and ADR 005.
- Evidence reorganised into dated folders with SHA-256 hashes (`docs/evidence/README.md`).
- `crci-node --listen` carries origin-signed `WireMessage` frames over TCP and runs every received message
  through `NodeRuntime` (signature check, replay window, strikes, bans). Each frame names the node that sent
  it separately from the message's author. The frame format is not compatible with earlier versions.
- `crci-node --send` and `--byzantine` send signed messages; `--byzantine` adds the `bad_signature`,
  `unsigned` and `unsigned_mce` modes.
- The 5-second severity-median check in `crci-node` only counts outliers; it no longer publishes
  placeholder divergence alerts.

### Fixed
- A validly signed message under another message's ID suppressed that message for good: the receiver
  treated the genuine one as a copy, whether it came as gossip or as a sync answer. So did a frame rejected
  for its key. The seen cache and the rescue store now key messages by signing key and ID. Found while
  writing the TLA+ model; TLC reproduces it with the old key.
- A sync answer that overtook the gossip copy of the same message got an honest neighbour struck for the
  copy. Answers go to the address in the request and gossip to the address configured for the peer, and when
  those differ they take different connections. One gossip copy that follows its accepted answer is now
  excused, within the same limit of one excuse per message per request; the model now holds with links that
  reorder frames. A node's own message is also queued for its neighbours under the node lock.
- The testbed analysis counted a ban of any attacker other than the first as an honest false eviction in
  runs with several attackers; it now excludes every attacker.
- A request for a sync after a relay link resumed is spaced 8 s from the previous request, counted after it was
  sent. It was spaced 6 s from before the dial, so a request delayed by TCP retransmissions got the next one
  refused by the peer's 5 s cooldown, and the node waited for the next 30 s round (longest delivery 47.6 s).
- A relay connection aborted more than 6 s after a write is now noticed (window 15 s), so its frames are
  recovered within seconds; such an abort used to wait for the next 30 s anti-entropy round.
- Sync requests and chain-head announcements no longer take sequence numbers from the origin's messages, which
  pushed its messages out of the replay window and out of what neighbours keep for anti-entropy. They have a
  counter and a window of their own.
- A node no longer accepts and relays its own message when a neighbour's echo arrives after the seen-ID cache was
  cleared; its neighbours used to strike it for the repeat.
- Frames lost with an aborted or closed relay connection are recovered within seconds instead of at the
  next 30 s anti-entropy exchange. A node whose relay to a peer failed, or whose connection died soon
  after a write, reconnects at once (also when it has nothing more to send) and opens the new
  connection with a `link_resumed` frame; the peer then asks it for a sync as soon as its answer
  cooldown allows (8 s after the previous request). Lost frames are still never re-sent by the
  sender, so no honest repeat can be struck.
- A relay connection no longer stalls behind TCP's retransmission backoff under heavy packet loss. On
  Linux and Android a dialed connection whose data stays unacknowledged for 5 s is aborted
  (`TCP_USER_TIMEOUT`) and re-established for the next frame. Before, at 20 % loss one connection could
  deliver nothing for up to 38 s, together with the sync answers meant to repair it.
- Honest neighbours are no longer banned for late sync answers under packet loss. A sync answer is tagged
  with the request it answers (covered by the TCP frame signature, removed on receipt), and only a tagged
  answer to a request this node sent to that neighbour, for a message the request did not list as held, is
  excused, once, within 120 s. Before, a request delayed by retransmissions was answered after the
  requester's 10 s session had expired, and the answer's repeats of already-relayed messages were struck
  as replays (measured at 10–20 % loss); and before that, a 30 s session renewed by the 30 s sync loop
  let a neighbour repeat every message it relayed once without a strike. Untagged repeats are no longer
  excused at all.
- One origin can no longer take the whole network's capacity by flooding fresh, validly signed
  messages. Each node accepts at most 20 new messages per origin in a burst, then 5 per second; the
  budget is charged after the signature and pinned-key checks, and excess messages are dropped without a
  strike and fetched again later by anti-entropy. Before, every neighbour admitted its full budget from
  the flooder and every honest node relayed it: on the testbed (50 nodes, one node flooding 40 messages/s)
  honest delivery fell to 82–98 % and the flooder was never struck.
- A repeat of a message from the same sender is judged before the sender's rate limit, so it is struck
  even when the sender is over budget. Before, a sender that kept its budget empty had its replays
  dropped by the rate limit without a strike.
- A copy of a message already received from another neighbour is dropped before the rate limit and the
  signature check. Before, every such copy spent a token and a signature check, so honest gossip
  redundancy alone exhausted the per-neighbour budget.
- `crci-node` no longer delays start-up while probing its peers: the probes run concurrently with a 2 s
  timeout. Before, each unreachable peer was probed in turn (about 21 s each on Windows) before the node
  processed any traffic.
- A node announces its chain head only when it changed. Before, it re-announced an unchanged head to every
  peer after every delivered message, doubling the frames on every link.
- Message and identity signatures are checked with strict Ed25519 verification: a small-order public key
  with a fixed signature previously verified for every message.
- A node listening on `0.0.0.0` or `::` warns that anti-entropy is off instead of sending sync requests
  whose answers could not reach it.
- Messages of type `mce` are no longer accepted without a valid signature.
- Idle peers are no longer banned: inactivity decay stops at the ban threshold, and only a strike bans.
- Packets older than the replay window are dropped without a strike; the W = 64 sliding window is always
  on, and the sequence number is covered by the origin's signature.
- Signature checks are bounded per sender by the token bucket instead of a lifetime cap of five per
  claimed origin, which dropped every later message from an honest node.
- The origin's signature covers every `WireMessage` field (note, message type, confidence, flags,
  visibility and sequence number included) in a length-prefixed, domain-separated encoding. Before, it
  covered only the ID, origin, severity and sequence number joined with `:`, so a relay could change the
  other fields, and IDs containing `:` were ambiguous. Signatures made by earlier versions do not verify.
- TCP connections start with a mutual Ed25519 challenge-response, and every frame is signed by the dialer
  for the session and numbered. Strikes and bans attach to the dialer's authenticated identity (declared
  node ID plus proved key) instead of a self-declared sender ID, so a peer can no longer get another node
  struck by claiming its ID, and frames cannot be injected, altered or replayed on the path.
  `TcpTransport::bind` takes the node's signing key. The frame format is not compatible with earlier
  versions.
- A message whose origin is pinned to another public key is dropped without a strike on the relay. The
  receiver cannot tell which key is genuine, and the strike let an adversary that announced an honest
  node's ID first get that node's honest relays banned.
- Property-based tests (proptest) of the replay window: with reordering depth at most W, every honest
  message is accepted once and no relay loses reputation; at any depth, no honest relay is struck.
- Reputation recovery depends on elapsed time only (0.0005 per second, one strike forgiven in 300 s)
  and never raises a score above the initial 0.5; two strikes within 300 s cap recovery at 0.18. Before,
  recovery grew with the peer's own traffic, so an adversary sending valid messages between replays was
  never banned, and good behaviour could be banked for a burst of up to six replays. Idle decay is
  removed: inactivity no longer changes a score. Banned peers recover at the same rate and are readmitted
  at 0.25.
- `recovery_bench` rewritten: exact duplicate replays on a simulated clock, sweeping the replay period.
- Anti-entropy after a partition: a node asks a neighbour for what it missed with a signed digest
  (`sync_request`), and the neighbour answers with up to 16 recent messages the digest lacks, at most
  once per 5 s per requester and about once per second in total. Answers
  that cross with ordinary gossip are not struck; unsolicited duplicates still are. `crci-node` requests
  from its `--peers` at start-up, every 30 s, and within 5 s of an unreachable peer coming back.
  `MeshSimulator` gains `disconnect` and `heal`. `TcpTransport::dial` opens an authenticated connection
  and reports the listener's identity.
- A node no longer announces its chain head after a pass that only received announcements; two flooding
  neighbours used to echo announcements until their rate limits dropped real messages.
- `crci_version()` reports the crate version instead of a hard-coded `0.1.0`.
- The TCP transport no longer panics on clock errors and logs through `log` instead of standard output.
- `/chain/head` on a TCP node reports the node's Merkle chain head instead of the latest message ID.

### Removed
- The mdBook (`docs/book/`) and its workflow, `docs/fault-model.md`, `docs/chaos/README.md`,
  `docs/dashboard.html`, `docs/architecture.mmd`, the unused `docker/` Prometheus/Grafana config,
  one-off scripts, and two benchmark files that were never compiled.
- Tracked build products (`libcrci_core.so`) and TLC scratch state.
- The legacy environment-variable-driven `node` binary and `scripts/run_proof.ps1`.
- The `paper_v2` build feature (the sliding window is always on).
- The unused `--config` and `--argon2-memory` command-line options.

## [2.1.0] - 2026-09-29

### Added
- `ablation_bench` (window size vs. reorder depth) and `recovery_bench` (paced attack) harnesses.
- `CITATION.cff` with the preprint DOI.

### Changed
- Version bumped to 2.1.0; documentation reframed around local misbehaving-peer isolation.
- Benchmark CSVs frozen; bench targets no longer run under `cargo test`.

### Fixed
- Seen-message cache eviction (BFT-046) that caused a test failure.
- Clippy warnings under `-D warnings`.

## [2.0.0] - 2026-09-15

### Changed
- Cargo workspace split into `crci-core` and `crci-node`.

### Added
- 5-node network-namespace WAN smoke test in CI.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-06-09

Initial tagged release: node runtime with reputation scoring, replay filter, TCP transport, HTTP API,
Android FFI bindings and the web dashboard.
