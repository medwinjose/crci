In-process simulation runs. Criteria written 2026-11-04, after timing runs of seed 1 on the build
before the control-window fix (cross N = 20/50/100, scale N = 5000) and before any run of the build
recorded here. If a criterion fails: say so, find the cause; no change to the criteria after the data.

Cross-check (group cross, N = 20/50/100, rgg and mesh, seeds 1-3, one attacker) against the testbed
runs of the same (kind, N, seed) in docs/evidence/testbed-fast-recovery-2026-11-04/r4/results.csv
(binary 04ec7be5; no loss, 10 ms per hop):
- same topology instance: mean_deg, max_deg, diam, aspl, clust equal;
- same attacker coverage: banned_by equal (every honest neighbour);
- false evictions: 0 in both;
- delivery before the fault and after the ban: both 1.0;
- downstream delivery: all delivered in both (message counts may differ by the few messages that
  fall between the fault and the last ban, because the period boundaries are not identical);
- strike dynamics: median eviction, simulated vs testbed wall clock, within 200 ms. This compares
  when the third strike falls; it is not a latency result of the simulation.

Scale (group scale, N = 100/500/1000/2000/5000, rgg and mesh, seeds 1-3, one attacker and
f = 0/10/20/30/45 % attackers):
- 0 honest false evictions and 0 strikes on honest senders in every run;
- every honest neighbour of every attacker bans it in every run (coverage 100 %);
- eviction stays local: 0 records of non-neighbours in any node's guard, in every run;
- delivery after the bans = 1.0 among honest nodes in the origin's component, in every run;
- f = 0 and one attacker: 0 rate-limit and origin-budget drops, delivery 1.0;
- partition rate and delivery over all honest nodes: reported as measured.

Long (group long, mesh N = 300 and rgg N = 150, 8 messages/s for 15 simulated minutes, f = 0 and
10 %, seeds 1-3; passes the seen-ID limit of 5000 and, on the mesh, the digest limit of 256 origins):
- 0 honest false evictions and 0 strikes on honest senders;
- f = 0: delivery 1.0;
- f = 10 %: every honest neighbour bans every attacker at least once; readmissions reported as
  measured;
- per-node state reported as measured against those limits.

Changed before any long run finished: the long group was first written with N = 500. Those processes
grew past 2 GiB each and four at once exhausted the 7.8 GiB of this WSL2 host (kernel OOM kill), with
no outcome recorded. N = 300 on both topologies reached 7 MiB per node after 80 s of wall-clock time and
was stopped, also with no outcome recorded. Per-node state grows with time and degree, so the
sizes above are what fits: the mesh keeps the digest limit in play (300 origins > 256); rgg at
N = 150 does not reach it.

Reported, no pass/fail: runtime and peak memory per N.

Testbed re-check of the two protocol fixes these runs led to, written before the run (real crci-node
processes, scripts/testbed/run_attacks.sh, the same 30 runs and the same limits as the fast-recovery
criteria, build recorded in binary.sha256):
- fidelity: the analyze.py checks hold in every run;
- over-budget refill5 and refill20: B banned by all neighbours, 0 honest bans;
- flood40: delivery while flooding >= 0.99, 0 honest drops, 0 honest bans;
- eventual (20 % loss, 60 s drain): delivery after the ban 1.0, 0 honest bans, B banned by all
  neighbours, max post-ban honest delivery latency < 30 s in every run, p99 <= 4.7 s.
The main, load and netem sweeps are not repeated for this build. They are repeated on the final
protocol build before submission; their published numbers remain those of binary 04ec7be5.

Result of that re-check: every criterion held except one. p99 was 6 687 ms (limit 4 700 ms; previous
build 3 361 ms) with max 22 055 ms (previous 25 299 ms). The criterion failed as written, and this is
recorded as such. Messages with a delivery over 3 s: 90 of 596 against 86 of 566, so the difference may be
the weight of a few messages lost near their source (one counts up to 96 deliveries).

Decision rule, written before more data: one fresh set of the same 18 runs on each build, old
(04ec7be5) first, then new. The fixes caused a latency regression if both new sets have a p99 above both
old sets and a higher share of messages with a delivery over 3 s in both. Then the fix is reworked.
Otherwise the p99 limit is recorded as failed once and the spread between sets of one build is reported
as what this metric can resolve.

Result of the decision rule: no latency regression from the two fixes. p99 old 3 361 and 4 067 ms, new 6 687
and 3 815 ms; messages with a delivery over 3 s old 15.2 and 13.9 %, new 15.1 and 13.1 %. The same p99 on one
build moved by up to 2.9 s between sets, which is what this metric can resolve.

The same sets found a separate, older defect. The second new set has 2 runs over the 30 s limit (worst
34 144 ms, 5 messages). Traced: the frames were written into a connection that TCP aborted after the
relay task's 6 s window, so nobody reconnected and they waited for the 30 s anti-entropy round. Failing test
first, window raised to 15 s, in transport/tcp.rs only; NodeRuntime is unchanged, so the simulation
results stand.

Criteria for the build with that fix, written before its runs (run_attacks.sh, then one more set of the 18
eventual runs):
- every criterion of the testbed re-check above, on the attack set;
- max post-ban honest delivery latency < 30 s in every eventual run of both sets;
- fidelity checks, 0 honest bans, B banned by all neighbours, delivery after the ban 1.0, in both sets;
- p99 reported per set against 4.7 s. This limit failed once on the previous build, and a value inside the
  3.4-6.7 s spread of the earlier sets is reported as it is, not as an improvement or a regression.

Result on that build (00e4b508): every pass/fail criterion held on both sets. Worst delivery 24 302 and
29 875 ms (limit 30 s, the second by 125 ms). p99 was 9 019 and 6 853 ms, both over 4.7 s and both outside the
3.4-6.7 s spread above, a case the criteria did not cover.

Decision rule for that case, written before computing it. The post-ban pool depends on when the last ban falls
(rgg N = 50 holds 2 853 to 14 850 deliveries depending on the set), so the sets do not weigh the same runs
equally. Compare all honest deliveries of each run, before and after the ban, which the fixed traffic plan
keeps the same size. Regression if the two 00e4b508 sets both have a p99 above both 04ec7be5 sets, and a higher
share of deliveries over 5 s than both, and a run-level permutation test (36 runs against 36, statistic = mean
per-run share over 5 s) gives p < 0.05. Then the 15 s window is reworked and re-measured. Otherwise the
post-ban p99 is recorded as failed on this build, with the spread between sets, and no latency claim
tighter than that spread is made.

Result of that rule: not a regression by the rule. All deliveries: p99 5 658 and 5 644 ms (04ec7be5), 7 522 and
6 746 ms (00e4b508); share over 5 s 1.49 and 1.40 %, 2.03 and 1.78 %; permutation p = 0.19, not below 0.05. The
direction is against the new build and these runs cannot exclude a small cost.

The same analysis found a separate defect, older than these fixes. The longest delivery in any period was
47 610 ms (l20-rgg-20-s1, a leaf node, before the fault; the post-ban criteria do not cover it). Traced: its
first request arrived 2.7 s late through TCP retransmissions, and the request for the resumed link arrived 3.5 s
after that, inside the responder's 5 s cooldown. The responder refuses without saying so, so the node waited
for the next 30 s round. On every build 697-734 of about 27 000 requests per set are refused this way. The requester
spaced its request 6 s from before the dial, while the cooldown runs from the arrival. Failing test first;
the requester now spaces it 8 s from after the send. NodeRuntime's answers are unchanged, so the simulation
results stand.

Criteria for the build with that fix, written before its runs (run_attacks.sh, then one more set of the 18

  all neighbours, delivery after the ban 1.0, on both sets;
- max post-ban honest delivery latency < 30 s in every eventual run of both sets;
- refused requests (received - answered - budget refusals) at most 175 per set, a quarter of the 700 above;
- reported as measured, with all earlier sets: post-ban p99 against 4.7 s, p99 of all deliveries, longest delivery

Result on that build (b17f6f0c): the attack set and both eventual sets held every pass/fail line except one.
Faithful 18/18 on both sets, 0 honest bans, B banned by all neighbours, delivery after the ban 1.0, flood40 delivery
1.0 with 0 honest drops, refill5/20 banned by 10/10. Worst post-ban delivery 17 458 and 20 103 ms (limit 30 s).

The refused-requests line failed as written: 365 and 403 against a limit of 175. Cause: the count was wrong, not
the node. It includes requests from the attacker to neighbours that had banned it, which are ignored on purpose
(364 and 399). Requests from honest nodes left unanswered: 313, 324 (04ec7be5), 358, 336 (00e4b508), then 1 and 4
on this build. The sentence above, "697-734 of about 27 000 requests per set are refused this way", overstated the
defect by about a factor of two for the same reason. The limit is not changed; the line stays failed as written and
the breakdown is reported next to it.

Reported as measured, all sets, 20 % loss, 60 s drain: post-ban p99 3 598 and 5 519 ms (limit 4 700 ms: the second
fails it, inside the 3.4-9.0 s spread of all sets), p99 of all deliveries 5 437 and 6 317 ms, share of deliveries
over 5 s 1.30 and 1.79 %, longest delivery in any period 33 459 and 28 201 ms.
